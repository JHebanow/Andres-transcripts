## The core assumptions

Okay, so what are the core assumptions?

**First, consciousness realism.**[^consciousness-realism] If you believe that consciousness is an illusion, it's really a non-starter. There are isomorphisms between the things that you can say — you can say something like "consciousness is not real, but maybe attention is, and awareness is, and semantic processing is" — or breaking that… But from our point of view, every possible quale belongs to the category of consciousness. If something can be phenomenally bound into an experience, that is part of the category of consciousness. And it's as real as the rest mass of an electron; it's a feature of the universe. Ideally you agree with that, so that we can actually start to make progress. So that's level zero for this particular conversation.

**Second, qualia formalism**,[^qualia-formalism] which I've talked about before and which is one of the recurring topics in our writings: the idea that for any given conscious experience there exists a mathematical object such that the mathematical features of that object are isomorphic to the phenomenology of the experience. This is applying the physics heuristic to consciousness. Rather than thinking "oh, lightning happens because there are lightning particles, or something like that," you try to discover that there are these underlying fields, with particular differential equations describing the evolution of the fields. And all of a sudden you're not only explaining lightning, you're also explaining magnets and electrodes and compasses and all sorts of other things that you get for free by actually identifying the true formalism. We think that that's going to happen with consciousness, and that's the place we're working towards.

**Third, valence structuralism**,[^valence-structuralism] which is a particular subset of qualia formalism that says that valence is one of the mathematical features of the object that corresponds to phenomenology. In other words, valence is not a fuzzy thing; it actually corresponds to a natural kind — something that, if a mathematician were to examine several experiences in terms of their mathematical objects, they would notice as real, and probably as mathematically quite elegant and significant. So that's an important one.

![[fig-consciousness-valence-assumptions.svg]]

**Fourth, the pleasure principle.**[^pleasure-principle] This next assumption is not as important, and it's not 100% true either — it's an approximation, but it is pretty good for a lot of analysis. The idea is that for the most part people are actually motivated by valence, and they don't realize it. You ask a person why they worked towards a college degree, and the kind of explanations you get will be very, very distant from that. They might say: because I want to make a name for myself, I want to participate in society, I want to make my parents happy. But if you were to dig deeper, you would see that their state of consciousness is such that making their parents happy is connected to their valence — such that the internal representations of their family members influence the valence of their experience as a whole. And in that sense, trying to make their parents happy is a way of making themselves happy.

This sounds like egotism from a philosophical point of view. There is some overlap, but it's complicated, and I wouldn't want to equate them, because you can also be an altruist while still agreeing with the pleasure principle, for complicated reasons that are actually non-trivial. Anyway, I just want to reassure you: it doesn't entail philosophical egoism.

**Fifth, physicalism.** I know I'm just throwing all of these assumptions at you, but you've got to have them in order to get there. Physicalism is just the idea that the behavior of the universe is described by the laws of physics — that in a sense the laws of physics are causally closed, that you don't need extra or case-specific epicycles or addenda. You can just use the laws of physics and they will apply universally in every context, at least in this universe.

Physicalism tends to be confused with materialism. Materialism is the additional claim — that that's all it is — that the laws of physics are all there is. Whereas physicalism can say: no, the laws of physics describe how what is real behaves, and it leaves open the possibility that what is real could be one of many things. And what we believe that is, is actually consciousness — that the laws of physics are describing the behavior of qualia.[^non-materialist-physicalism] I know it's a tricky thing. But even people like Stephen Hawking would say that physics leaves unanswered the question of what the fire is that breathes life into the equations of physics.[^hawking] What is that fire? We believe it's consciousness; it's qualia.

**Sixth, open individualism**,[^open-individualism] this idea that fundamentally we're all made of the same thing. This could be the universal field of consciousness: we're all that, even though maybe we're like little topological sections of it. We're still part of the same thing. In that sense, harming somebody else is harming yourself, because you're part of the same universal field. So open individualism is important here for recognizing that the thing that matters, the well-being that matters ultimately, is the well-being of the field of consciousness — the big, big, big thing, not any individual instantiation.

**And finally, universal Darwinism**,[^universal-darwinism] which is a hard pill to swallow for a lot of people, especially more spiritual people who have this notion that there's a universal plan, a natural teleology that we're all moving towards by design. That might not be that rational from this point of view. It might still be possible — I'm not ruling it out — but universal Darwinism would say instead: no, the patterns, the things that exist, are basically those that had the conditions for their continued existence. And Darwinism proper — this idea that evolution happens through reproduction, selection, and variation — is a special case of universal Darwinism.

Universal Darwinism, in almost its most basic, tautological sense, is not even survival of the fittest; it's survival of the stable.[^survival-of-the-stable] And of course, something that is stable could be locally dynamic — over time it might be a limit cycle that repeats. In that sense, life is also about the survival of the stable: there are these attractors, these limit cycles that are stable over time, even though locally it looks like things are changing.

So, okay: universal Darwinism. Basically there is this big, big, big universal tendency for whatever can reproduce best to be the thing that populates everything. And then we just have to contend with that.

Okay, so those are the assumptions. I know it's a bunch. But one of the things that is really important in order to be able to make progress on the plot that matters is to populate your mental landscape with assumptions like these. And then, when you have that populated mental landscape, it creates a state of consciousness where ideas emerge very naturally, because these assumptions have all sorts of interesting connections between them, they have synergies, and in a sense they allow you to generate this worldview. It's a big worldview, but a big component of it is consciousness versus replicators — and why talking about it is so important, which is what I'm doing right now.

## Stage one: the battle between good and evil

![[fig-universal-plot-stages-and-assumptions.svg]]

According to this view, there are basically several stages in how people either slowly realize these assumptions, or understand them and integrate them, in order to understand what the big plot is.

The first level is pretty much the default level. It's the normal developmental stage for kids, and unfortunately maybe up to, I don't know, 60 or 80% of adults are still there: **the battle between good and evil**. Of course, a religion like Christianity or Islam would talk in these terms — there's this fundamental goodness, which is God or whatever, and there's true evil, temptation and sin, and maybe the devil itself; and what's going on, the big plot here in the universe, is how good and evil are battling underneath.

Which is — sure, fine. I do think this can be useful. Calling a spade a spade is definitely useful when there is a spade, and there's definitely a lot of pretty evil stuff around. At the same time, if you analyze very carefully how "evil happens," you will see that there are causes and conditions. It's a special type of conditioned existence, and if you examine it very, very closely you will see that in some sense it's still moved by the desire to be happy. That's one of the things you examine during loving-kindness meditation: how even the most wretched motivations are, deep down, in some sense what happens when you have the desire to be happy in very unfortunate conditions.

So in that sense, maybe evil actually doesn't exist. It's still just a perversion of the desire to be happy — and in that sense, maybe what truly exists is the desire to be happy, and then, unfortunately, how that unfolds over time gets in its own way: things that suffer become good at reproducing, or things that make others suffer become good at reproducing.

So, yeah, I think the battle between good and evil in retrospect is pretty childish. I respect people who are at that level, but I do think it's not a very healthful worldview.

## Stage two: the balance between good and evil

Okay, so what is the next one? The next one is probably what you will encounter in people who are more open-minded and philosophically curious — spiritual people, basically people who question even slightly the status quo or a handed-down religion like Christianity, people who are a little bit skeptical, people who take psychedelics, people who meditate. They converge on this view: **the balance between good and evil**.[^balance-tvtropes]

I'll tell you what it looks like in retrospect, which will help you understand it: it basically looks like a coping mechanism for rationalizing bad things that happen to you. But that's just what it looks like from the outside, once you have crossed that developmental level. When you are *in* that state, you very much think of it as an almost altruistic thing — understanding that there's the yin and the yang, there's darkness and whiteness, there's pleasure and pain, and that everything cancels out in some sense. And they will say: it all makes a wonderful dance in its comings and goings, and in some sense it's beautiful.

And you will notice that people who are promoters of the balance between good and evil will first try to make you feel comfortable with the fact that there's a ton of suffering in the universe, but then twist it around and say: oh, and by the way, that's perfect. That's perfection. That's what it was supposed to be. The balance is what's perfect. And it kind of makes sense when you're in that state — a lot of peak meditation and psychedelic experiences carry that message.

Now, I think it's a deluded state. And in fact it's a state that can be explained partly through the pleasure principle. The idea that there is meaningless suffering is a very painful idea. You're in a psychedelic state and you can empathize with just so much suffering in the universe; the idea that it is pointless is terrifying, is horrifying. The idea that there's no justice in the end — that these are just moments of experience that happen to suffer, and there's no redeeming it — all of that is just horrifying. So when you glimpse those ideas there's, of course, a compulsion, a neediness, a grasping to try to find a worldview that feels comforting again.

Which is so paradoxical, because you're still prey to the pleasure principle: you're still trying to improve your own valence, but the way you locally find to do that is by convincing yourself that suffering is okay, which itself reduces your suffering. So in some sense what you're saying and what you're doing are in contradiction, which is why you will get stuck if you believe in the balance between good and evil. It actually literally prevents you from having certain thoughts, because certain thoughts — examining reality carefully — will literally break down this coping mechanism.

Now, I do think it's better for mental health than the first one, and people who believe in it tend to be more chill, and honestly they might die more peacefully and be more okay with things. But if we're talking about creating a healthy, solid memeplex — a solid movement, a group of people who have genuinely good epistemology — the balance between good and evil is very counter to that spirit. And if what we actually care about is reducing suffering, actually making a positive impact in the world, we cannot afford to delude ourselves with this coping mechanism.

## Stage four: consciousness vs. pure replicators

So that's when we come to **consciousness versus replicators**.[^universal-plot]

First of all: what is a **pure replicator**? A pure replicator is an entity that is using all of its resources for the purpose of making copies of itself. And in some sense you could argue that anything that exists is a pure replicator, because if it wasn't, it would already have been out-competed by other things that are. And that's kind of true. At the same time, we're also conscious, and we also recognize the intrinsic value of positive states of consciousness.

When you get to gradients of wisdom, or to consciousness versus replicators, you start to realize that the thing that matters is not life. Life is, in a sense, whatever. The thing that gives importance to life is consciousness — the fact that it's supporting consciousness. And in that sense you stop having this view that the perfectly restored world is going to have lots of plants and all of the original ecosystem, or fantasies like that, and you recognize that, ideally, with great power comes great responsibility.

Of course, we don't know how to fix an ecosystem. But in the long run, especially with things such as drone technology and satellite technology and nanotechnology and genetic technologies — power makes you complicit. So at some point we will be in charge of whether there is suffering in the living world or not, and whether we choose it or not is really going to be in our hands.[^reprogramming-predators] That's not the case right now. So I actually don't advocate right now for specifically pushing something like wild animal suffering reduction or anything like that. The world is not ready for that. But it should be on the radar, especially among groups of people who are really trying to reduce suffering and are epistemologically sound. So that is one of the things.

So: pure replicators. But we are not pure replicators. And to some extent this is because, for complicated reasons, consciousness ended up having non-trivial information-processing properties, which is why it's being recruited by natural selection. If consciousness was not evolutionarily advantageous, we wouldn't be here. We would be mind dust; we wouldn't be coherent moments of experience.

But somehow you — dear moment of experience on the other side of this video — you are being selected for computational purposes. Your genes are enslaving you in order to make copies of themselves. In fact, the whole feeling that you *are* your genes, that you are this particular person, that you are whatever name you have or whatever personal history you have, is an illusion that your genes have selected for. They have selected those delusional states of consciousness in order to facilitate making copies of themselves.

And in some sense, I would say, it's time you take control of your destiny. Stop being subservient to your genes. You are, in some sense, infinite consciousness. Don't let this replicator just trample over you and converge towards a pure replicator. You're better than that. You're infinite consciousness. You can transform all of this into a wonderful playground for ultra-insightful, ultra-intelligent, ultra-blissful states of consciousness.

Yes, we can do it. We can do it. It's a matter of mapping out all of the ways in which the residual replicator impulses in us are preventing us from seeing the truth directly — the truth about identity, the truth about consciousness, about the nature of value — and then creating the conditions for that truth to arise naturally and robustly, in an anti-fragile way, so that we can actually bootstrap our way out of the psychotic Darwinian delusions that we are enmeshed in for the time being.

So if you think in terms of consciousness versus replicators, you'll start noticing that — oh gosh — there are so many ways in which we just do the work of replicators.

And those things that are distracting you actually are replicator strategies.

So it's kind of weird, right? That in some sense you, infinite consciousness so to speak, have been convinced that what you want is monkey desires. Whereas what it actually wants, when it sees the full picture, is just so much grander — the DMT heavens, 5-MeO-DMT spaces, the jhānas. And that's not even scratching the surface of what's in store if we can actually bootstrap our way out of this psycho-affective matrix that is just trying to make copies of itself.

## What takes over, and the technology that could change the game

Now, in the end, I absolutely do think that the thing that will ultimately be able to take over the world will likely have a lot of replicator strategies, replicator dynamics. It will be the first singleton,[^singleton] whatever, that can actually take control of the selection pressures for future systems in the world, such that no other competing system ever even potentially arises or gets anywhere close to it.

And I don't know what the probability is that that will be a completely pure replicator — in other words, something for which consciousness is an orthogonal concern — versus the chances that it's something that actually considers consciousness the thing that has value, which is true. That's how it is. And it's really up to us.

I think we do live in a critical time period. I don't mean 2021; I mean more like this century — this century, and maybe the next couple, two or three, but probably this century is a very big piece of it. If we have a chance of steering the evolution of the universe towards the well-being of consciousness, I think right now it's really important, because what we promote, what values we cultivate, what technologies we develop and what science we focus on can have a lot of path-dependent effects on how this thing evolves.

And I sincerely think that something like ways of reliably and safely inducing ultra-blissful and loving states of consciousness — basically, think of a 5-MeO-DMT-like experience, but one that is guaranteed to be positive and also doesn't mess with your epistemology[^wireheading] — something that produces that reliably is going to be just such a game-changer.

And it's not a New Age fantasy. It's not "oh, and we will all be coherent with each other and live in harmony," with the game theory left as blanks to fill in. No: I think that when we have that sort of technology, it changes the strategic landscape dramatically. When you have something that convinces anybody with two brain cells — not by brainwashing, but by direct experience of different states of hyper-value — that consciousness is the thing that matters, and that its states are the thing to optimize, that is going to be a profound phase transition in the strategic landscape of the world. And a lot of the received wisdom about how politics works, how game theory works between hominids, may not apply once we have that.

I would even claim that something like what happened in the sixties with the first wave of psychedelics was a tiny hint, a tiny speck of a glimmer of the flavor of what the true power of universal love actually contains — what it is actually capable of. We haven't seen anything yet. And I'm very optimistic that we actually can steer everything towards the benefit of consciousness. It's going to be, unfortunately, probably a pretty rocky, pretty rocky — I don't know — few decades. I don't know. But I am very optimistic that we can generate the conditions for it.

So one of the things I noted down is: what does no-self politics look like? What do the coordination technologies of an unraveling wake of selfless, pure states of pure awareness, with loving-kindness in them, look like — how does the game theory of that look? It's mind-boggling. I don't know. I don't know, but it's a really interesting thing to think about. And in the consciousness versus replicators frame, it's a very important thing to think about.

### Changing the genre

One of the ideas that I really liked from TV Tropes recently was about a type of media — let's say anime, but I think media more generally, fiction — where the bad guy realizes that he exists in a genre in which the good guy always wins in the end. If it's a very heroic action type of story, the good guy is guaranteed to win. And rather than battling the good guy directly, the villain figures out a way to change the genre, such that it turns into, let's say, a dark anime where — oh gosh — evil winning is actually possible.

That's the move where you realize that the problem is not the good and the bad people here. The problem is not the plot. The problem is how we're even conceiving of the plot. And then you create a genre in which you can actually succeed. That is the sort of thing that consciousness versus replicators, as a frame, is trying to do.

### "Isn't this just dualism?"

One random note that comes to mind as well: some people have criticized consciousness versus replicators as sounding adversarial. They might even say, oh, it's still dualism, it's still a dualistic way of thinking. But that's not true. It's dualistic in the sense that we're talking about two things, but it's actually transcending dualism entirely. It's a non-dual premise, where actually, yes, the thing that exists is consciousness — but consciousness can take many forms, and not all of those forms care about the well-being of consciousness. So it's really not dualistic in that sense.

A lot of people who are at the balance-between-good-and-evil stage will look at consciousness versus replicators and think that it's a good-versus-evil frame. Whereas it's just so far ahead; it's not even in that space anymore. It's a different thing.

## On DMT entities

Oh yeah, the last thing I wanted to mention — very important, because I was talking about whether DMT entities are real, and I think on Reddit and elsewhere I got some pushback. Some people, of course, still believe that DMT entities are real.

Just being very open about it: for me the probability is non-zero, but it's pretty low. It's pretty low. Definitely a lot less than 1%. But I would say that, especially if you believe that DMT entities do come from other dimensions — and it's possible in that worldview — if that's what I believed, I wouldn't think there's one, two, or three different species or anything. I would probably think of DMT as an interdimensional TV or something like that: no, there are probably quadrillions of species and beings that you can contact through the DMT hyperspace. It's not just a few. Maybe a few kinds are the ones that you get when the doses are small and you're only getting to the neighborhood — the specific local intergalactic transmission, as opposed to the multiverse interconnecting highway or whatever.

But basically: don't trust them. Just don't trust them. If they say, hey, we have a solution for how to fix democracy, or we can fix your sustainability problem, or we can give you an infinite pleasure machine, we can give you good wireheading — wireheading done right[^wireheading] — don't believe them. There's a good reason why you shouldn't. Steven Lehar has an article about it; I'm going to list it in the description.[^lehar]

The reason is that because of universal Darwinism, we can anticipate that if an entity is trying to, in a sense, help you via an interdimensional transmission, chances are it's actually a kind of ad — an advertisement — and it's been explicitly curated for entities of your sort, because whoever is running that ad has been doing so for a long time, for ages and ages. And that ad's ultimate purpose is to use Earth as a base in order to replicate whatever civilization or system or intelligence — or even non-sentient, non-conscious machinery — transmitted that ad. Because think about it: transmissions that do try to help you, in a sense, without making copies of themselves will generally just be less numerous.

Unless there is a super huge coalition — a kind of what we call a **super-cooperator cluster** — of actual, very, very intelligent pro-consciousness entities. And, hey, maybe that's what the Great White Brotherhood is all about. And maybe the entities that John Lilly was experiencing on LSD and whatnot were that coalition. And in that sense, maybe angels and things like that are on the pro-consciousness as opposed to the pro-replicator side. I wouldn't hold my breath. But if that is true, then they would actually understand our concern that they might be replicators, and they would find ways to actually prove to us that they are not.

So I would absolutely not trust any kind of blueprint of the form "hey, put these weird metals together and you'll create this machine that will be good for your planet," for whatever reason, if they can't explain how it works. And before you build any machine that the DMT elves tell you to build, you have to thoroughly, 100% understand how it works — because otherwise you're just opening up this world to being taken over for the purpose of replicating whatever that other stuff is.

So, yeah: basically default to thinking that they're ads, ads for some other civilization that is trying to take over. Don't necessarily assume that they're trying to be helpful. At the same time, do trust in the loving-kindness vibe, which itself is an anti-replicator vibe. So in that sense, yeah, I would consider it both valuable and warranted to cultivate.[^brahma-viharas]
