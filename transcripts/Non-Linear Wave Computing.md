## What are non-linear waves?

Let's break down the title of the video: non-linear wave computing — vibes, gestalts, and realms. Take the first component first: what are non-linear waves?

We're sold this story in high school that what's called the **superposition principle** applies to waves in general. If you drop a rock in a lake, and another rock in the same lake a couple of seconds later, you see the waves propagate — and they superimpose, but they continue traveling uninterrupted. In a sense they're not interacting with one another. They can superimpose, and that's totally fine.

A lot of waves do behave that way: sound waves within a reasonable energy regime — what's called the linear energy regime — most water waves within a reasonable energy regime, light as well, and many other phenomena follow the superposition principle. But that is not a law of nature. Not all waves behave that way.

You've probably encountered non-linear waves in the physical world around you. If you've ever been to the beach, there's this very interesting phenomenon where you get a confluence of a lot of waves, and sometimes two large waves collide with one another, form a huge column, and then — it's almost time-symmetric — they de-collide, and it moves backwards. That's not the principle of superposition going on there. The conditions for that particular phenomenon are that the waves have to be pretty tall — in other words, highly energized; how tall the wave is will be associated with the total energy of the wave — and it has to be in shallow water. There are some interesting interactions that happen there where all of a sudden the waves become non-linear.

Strictly speaking, waves in water are only *approximately* linear. They're never perfectly linear, because they're not actually perfectly sinusoidal waves. If you look at the water molecules, they have a weird movement that is *approximated* by sinusoidal waves — but that's not what they are. They're something else. Similarly: you may think that if you take two poles and hang a cord between them, the shape you get is a parabola. No, it's not a parabola. It's a catenary.[^catenary] There are a lot of things like this, where the actual mathematical structure is slightly different from what it looks like. Those differences may not matter very much in normal, everyday, room-temperature existence. But push it a little bit further, and oh my gosh — you start to get weird effects that you wouldn't have expected if your intuition were correct.

### Solitons

Technically speaking, there's a whole category of non-linear phenomena in water — and in other media as well — called **solitons**. Solitons are very weird. People had observed them in the past and didn't know what was going on. You can be in a lake and all of a sudden you see this blob of water traveling in a straight line. What *is* that? It doesn't make any sense; it's very unusual behavior for a wave. And they collide — and when they collide, weird things can happen, almost as if particles were colliding. It's a very strange wave–particle hybrid. (Ultimately everything in physics is a bit like that.)

So what's going on with solitons? The full technical description would be its own lecture, but approximately, you have a balance of two forces. One is **dispersion** — how quickly different frequencies, different spacings of the wave, travel in the medium. In water it turns out that small waves actually travel faster than large waves; that's dispersion. The other is the non-linearity. With a soliton, these two cancel each other out perfectly and form a stable equilibrium that can travel as a unit. It's very, very trippy, isn't it? The fast wave is trying to advance, but the non-linearity is pushing it back, and they're in perfect balance, and they just travel at a constant speed that way.

### Non-linear optics

Non-linearity also happens in other places — something Steven Lehar[^lehar] made me aware of. This is a huge topic: non-linear optics.

If you combine two lasers in a quartz crystal — or many other crystalline materials — they will form an interference pattern, where essentially you're over-energizing the precise interference pattern of the superposition of those two lasers. And something really weird happens: all of a sudden, that intersection point works as a **retroreflector**.[^phase-conjugation] Meaning that if you shine another laser at it, it doesn't reflect like a mirror — it reflects like a retroreflector, one of those things you put on your bicycle or that you find on cars, where wherever you shine the light, it bounces straight back to you. That happens with crystals and light.

So light is usually linear in its behavior, but in some media it can become very non-linear. The reason that happens is that electric fields can change the refractive index of materials.[^refractive-index] A laser, if it gets strong enough, is inducing an electric field, which changes the refractive index of the material. As a consequence, it's going to bend the light. So you get these weird effects called **self-focusing**, where you shine a laser at a certain energy regime and the laser just goes right through, no problem; you increase the energy, and all of a sudden it focuses down to a point, and it may explode the material, because it's just such a high temperature. All of that is non-linear optics, and those are non-linear waves.

## Vibe fundamentals

So what is a vibe? Vibes are real. Vibes are real. They're obviously a very important part of your experience. As I said, a very soft cushion will have a certain vibe that is very different from, say, punk-style spiky decorations that are scary and high-frequency, with pinch points. That's a very different vibe.

Start with the **symmetry theory of valence** proposed by Mike Johnson, and especially the concept of **valence structuralism** that he proposed in *Principia Qualia*[^principia-qualia]. The insight there is that the thing that makes your experience feel good or bad has to do with the *structure* of your experience.

So to a first approximation, what determines the vibe is the shape of the waves traveling in your nervous system, and how they're traveling. Both their shape and how they travel. And to a first approximation: the smoother the medium — the more symmetrical, the more smooth the geometry — the higher the valence, the more pleasant it's going to be. This quality of softness.

Klein — the mathematician famous for the Klein bottle — also formalized the concept of geometry in terms of symmetries.[^klein] (*I'll put a link in the comments, in the description.*) You can characterize a geometric space in terms of which symmetrical transformations leave its properties the same. And when you're in a yoga retreat, or in loving-kindness or highly concentrated states, your phenomenal energy body is going to be very smooth. The waves travel in an uninterrupted way, and as a consequence, they don't lose energy as they do so — there's an energy-preservation effect there — and they don't change their shape either.

So to a first approximation, how pleasant a vibe is has to do with how smooth, symmetrical, and geometrically regular the medium is within which it travels, and how regular the shapes of the waves are.

### Symmetry breaking

In contrast, the things that make a vibe unpleasant are essentially **symmetry-breaking operations**. In the most abstract way — and here's an awesome, awesome diagram — consider a hierarchy of symmetries [shown on screen: a square and its symmetry-broken descendants]. At the top, you have a square, which has all of the possible symmetries of a quadrilateral. As you break symmetries, you move down the hierarchy.

One of the descendants is interesting: it breaks the *rotational* symmetry — maybe it only rotates in one direction. So the square is the same under one set of transformations, but this shape is only symmetric under rotation in one direction. That's a weird, almost invisible way of breaking the symmetry, but it's definitely important mathematically.

There are other ways, of course. You can squish the square, and now you have a rhombus or a rectangle, and those have different symmetry elements, different possible transformations that leave them the same. With every symmetry-breaking operation you add, it becomes more and more irregular — all the way until it's completely irregular.

Again, to a first approximation: the more irregular it is, the more unpleasant the vibes of these things are going to be. Why? Because when you're representing this square, it's a combination of the non-linearities that create its borders and limitations, and those are sending waves of energy to the rest of your experience. If the square is perfectly symmetrical, those waves of energy will actually be regular. That's kind of crazy to contemplate.

To a first approximation, how much negative valence you're experiencing is going to be a weighted score of:

- how many **pinch points** the medium of wave propagation has;
- how many **shears** — areas where you have discontinuities, or stress in a symmetrically broken way, which dephases the structure;
- how many **blocks** — regions the waves outright don't travel through;
- and how many **reflectors** — parts where the wave travels and then just reflects back.

So if you have a stomach upset, or a knee pain, or whatever it may be, introspect on that. You'll realize that how unpleasant the sensation is, is approximated by how many pinch points, how many blockages, how many shears, and how many reflectors of the waves of energy it contains. And all of these aggregate into how much stress and strain is being generated in your entire system.

### Frequency and distance

Importantly, whenever you generate a non-linear structure — anything you're representing — the frequencies of the vibe of that thing will be related in an inverse way to its distances.

Consider a very long tube that you're representing in your mind. That is going to be emitting several kinds of vibes. First, there's a harmonic resonance structure along its length, which is going to be a very low frequency, because it's a long tube. But there are also waves *across* it, in its thickness. If it's a thin sheet — sure, it's very long, but if it's also very thin, that's going to be a very high-frequency component. So a long, thin sheet is going to have both a low-frequency and a high-frequency component.

So it's like a Fourier transform of all of these non-linear structures that you're representing. That's what the vibe generators in your experience are. And we're starting to see some elements with which to understand and decipher the vibe of a thing.

This inverse relationship between distance and frequency for generating waves is, by the way, an insight of Steven Lehar's. ## The attack, decay, sustain, release of vibes

Now let's get into the subtler details of vibes — a more advanced understanding. It's not only the frequencies that matter, the Fourier decomposition. The other thing that matters is what's called the **attack, decay, sustain, and release** of these waves: the envelope, which describes how high the wave goes and how long each phase takes. The first rise is the attack; the fall from the peak is the decay; the plateau it settles at is the sustain; and the final fade is the release.

What makes the sounds of different musical instruments different is not only the distribution of energy across their harmonics; it's also the attack, decay, sustain, and release of the notes.

It's one of these puzzling things, right? This is happening over time. Presumably your ear only has access to one point of it at a time. But obviously we're integrating that information over time and generating a representation that simultaneously contains temporal information — a kind of temporal coding. So all of this is being compressed. And I think this is being translated into the *shape* of the waves propagating in your world simulation.

What does the wave front look like? Is it a sharp attack — nothing there, and then all of a sudden it gets really high-energy? Or is it something softer, where it kind of has a warning, a build-up, and *then* it happens — a soft attack?

To a first approximation, friendlier vibes will have friendlier attacks: a friendly ramp-up of energy, as opposed to a shockwave that hits all of you at once. Very unfriendly energies will be full of shockwaves that are destructive, as opposed to trying to harmonize or merge the energies.

## Gestalts

Anyway, let's get into **gestalts**. Gestalts are what vibes construct.

When you're using energized waves and generating non-linearities, and you construct a scene — that would be a gestalt. People like Grossberg and Steven Lehar, and a lot of visual perception research, focus on gestalts: where all of the elements of a scene pop into becoming more than the sum of the parts.

So what is that? It's a resonance phenomenon. It always is a resonance phenomenon — but it's a *non-linear* resonance phenomenon. The elements of the scene are each emitting their own vibe, and when you get a gestalt, all of those vibes get interlocked. They phase-lock, and they click into place. That's a gestalt.

We tend to think of a gestalt as a snapshot of experience. But they're actually, I think, a little bit more interesting than that. I would say gestalts are **propositional**: they're scenes, or sentences, or situations. And that's how we reason. I think our brain reasons through sentences — not only is there *something*, that something is also *doing something*. That is what you're capturing; that's the full gestalt and its vibe. A story is a stringing-together of action-based gestalts to form a continuous movie, or set of actions. And all of those gestalts are in resonance with each other. Ultimately, when you create the movie, they form a macro-structure that is itself in a resonant configuration.

### Gestalt formation as search

For a lot of gestalts — especially if you're studying a technical field, or philosophy, or whatever it may be — I'd say the process is a search process. And it's a very interesting search process: the search uses wave computing.

Of course, you could emulate it with a Turing machine. But the runtime complexity would be off — and you'd be completely missing the point. Yes, of course you could simulate it with a digital computer. But you're missing out on being able to use the medium, the *physical* medium, to do a lot of the work for you.

In a recent video I talked about how intelligence is all about figuring out a self-organizing principle that will solve the problem for you. This is what we're talking about: recruiting the appropriate wave propagation media, which, when interacting with one another, generate a gestalt — a representation that allows the problem to be solved for you.

Importantly, from a physical point of view, every gestalt is going to be a **local energy minimum**. That's by design: oscillatory synchrony is energetically cheap. It's exactly the same principle as why soap bubbles become spherical — when they're not spherical, they wobble and radiate energy around them, so they fall into their local energy minimum. Gestalts are local energy minima because they're resonant configurations. But again, they're *non-linear* resonant configurations.

### The Magic Eye analogy

To find or understand a gestalt — and I think a lot of our reasoning, our thinking, even how we make sense of high-level structures of the world, like countries, or processes, even very abstract processes like annealing — these are fairly subtle gestalts that you need to fine-tune so they serve the purpose you want.

The process of doing that is essentially like looking at an autostereogram: a **Magic Eye**. *So — a Magic Eye. I have one here [holding up a Magic Eye image]. I don't know if you'll be able to do it through the camera, but you can just Google "Magic Eye."* The whole texture is the same.

I've talked about how there's one level of the DMT experience that is full of Magic Eye phenomenology. Yes — because, again, you're in a highly energized state of consciousness, and the local minimum will be gestalts made of Magic Eyes, of depth maps.

I think this is actually a metaphor Daniel Ingram used to refer to meditation states, like the **jhānas**.[^ingram] For a while, you're doing a lot of work and the things don't match, they don't match, they don't match. The curve of constructing a gestalt looks like that: you're trying to find a low-energy attractor that satisfies all these constraints, with vibe computing, non-linear wave computing.

## The valence imprint of a gestalt

So those are gestalts. Again, those are the bricks, the raw materials. You construct them with sets of vibes — but then the gestalts are things that, once you get them, once you stabilize them and construct them, you can use to build something else.

## Realms

And that takes me to what I want to say next: **realms**, the realms of experience. My claim is that they are collections of gestalts that are *self-reinforcing*. There are various attractors, and eventually, scientifically, we will figure out precisely what those attractors are. But in the meantime, we can talk about them in light of the Buddhist realms.

There are ten Buddhist realms — most people think of them as six, but it does expand to at least ten, and I like the ten version.[^ten-realms]

### Fabrication and defabrication

In order to construct a realm, there are two forces at play. Picture a graph, which he sketches on screen (reproduced below). The **y-axis** is your pre-existing **level of fabrication** — how fabricated your experience is, which is how many non-linearities you have stored: how many gestalts you contain.

And you *do* contain them. Every gestalt that you generate gets saved into your subconscious — the "storehouse consciousness," as they call it in Buddhism.[^storehouse] There's a ton of them. If you generate a particular gestalt, it will have a vibe and a mood. You may forget about it, but someday, randomly, you're dreaming, and — wow, that mood I had never experienced since I was four years old, when I saw that clown. That sort of thing happens. Or: I went to, I don't know, that museum. A fun memory. They will each have that unique consonance, dissonance, and noise signature.

So a particular realm is an **anti-fragile structure** made of a lot of gestalts interacting with one another.

The **x-axis** is the net rate of change: to the right, the **rate of fabrication**; to the left, the **rate of defabrication**. Where you sit on the x-axis determines how fast you move *vertically*:

- Far to the right — fabricating fast — and you're moving very fast **upwards**.
- Closer to the origin on the right, you're still fabricating, but you're moving up much more slowly.
- Cross to the left of the origin and you move in the opposite direction: you're **defabricating**, and your level of fabrication falls — slowly when you're near the origin, very fast when you're far out to the left.

At the origin itself, where nothing at all is being fabricated, that would be **nirodha samāpatti**,[^nirodha] the peak awakening states, where there's just absolutely nothing, or pure consciousness.

But most realms exist in the upper band of the graph, where the world is already pretty fabricated — and most people are going to be somewhere in there, in some kind of anti-fragile realm or another.

![The fabrication / defabrication graph](fabrication-defabrication.svg)

If you take 5-MeO-DMT — say, a small therapeutic dose to deal with trauma — the trauma will come up, and then it will shake itself apart and defabricate. So you start high up on the fabrication axis: you're really traumatized, so there are a lot of self-reinforcing fabrications. And 5-MeO-DMT throws you far over to the left — defabricating fast — so you drop quickly. But it may not take you all the way down. After the session you might still be quite high up, and you might need several more sessions to step down, one threshold at a time, until you finally get below the level at which the realm stops being anti-fragile — and you can exit that realm.

The thing is, if you don't get there, the realm may start to *refabricate* itself, because it's anti-fragile. That's the whole phenomenon of **re-traumatization**. Talk therapy can actually make something worse. So can psychedelic therapy: you can refabricate a trauma that you already dealt with, if it still has the seeds of it, and you're not skilled enough, or you're too stressed. If you're too stressed, that also can happen.

It doesn't need to be a moral thing. If you have too much adrenergic activity, or some medical problem, that will energize those representations, and again — there's a lot of moralistic thinking, I think, when it comes to how these things work. But no: I think there are a lot of very basic physical, physiological things that we can, ideally, be able to hack at some point.

So what are the realms — these anti-fragile attractors?

### The hungry ghost realm

We can start with the **hungry ghost**: that feeling of frustration, of never feeling satiated. Michael Taft, in a meditation about this, was saying: you know, *Pirates of the Caribbean*, when there are all these ghost pirates and they're eating a feast, but they're all made of bones, and the food just goes through them. You have intense cravings, but they don't satisfy.

Craving is a certain kind of gestalt that has a pulling, tightening quality. Rob Burbea says that if you introspect in meditation on your energy body and a craving comes up, you will realize that the intensity of the craving is directly proportional to the tension and the contraction in your energy body.[^burbea] So there's that duality there. And that generates a gestalt.

### The core of a realm

That's how you represent the world, the things that happen to you. But another thing about realms is what I would call the **core** of a realm: how you conceptualize your sense of self, how you interpret reality, what kind of lens you have when you're in that realm.

When you're a hungry ghost, your sense of self — which we can visualize as a sphere — is depressed, depressurized. There's a feeling that it needs something external to fill it up, and it's always half full. That's pretty unpleasant.

You'll also notice in meditation that there's a correspondence between the core, the sense of self, and your sense of the external environment. There's what they call **co-arising**.[^co-arising] I think that's very much true, and it has to do with these non-linear wave dynamics — because for you to energize an external representation, you need an internal source that emits the vibes that will generate it.

So think of the cores as the vibe emitters — the primary vibe emitter. And if you're really upset, your primary vibe emitter is going to be a harsh, harsh wave. Which takes us to the hell realm.

### The hell realm

The **hell realm** is an anti-fragile attractor of *dissonant* gestalts — gestalts that energize each other and are all unpleasant.

One of the very sad things about the failure modes of our nervous system is that there is no end to hell. You can make hells even worse. I'm sure there's a worst possible hell given a particular nervous system, but it's unfathomably worse than what you can imagine. So whenever there's a tricky situation, you have to think not only "how do I solve it?" but also "how do I prevent it from getting worse?" Because it can be ten times, or a hundred times, or a thousand times worse. And we're usually not very cognizant of that fact.

The core here is a self-repeating loop of "it's my fault," or "it's the world's fault," or "the world is horrible." And if you approach every situation with that vibe emitter, you're going to slowly be kindling and turning your world into a hell world as well. That's one of the reasons it's so difficult to get out of.

In many cases, to get out of hell you might need a small period of no pain and no disturbance, so you can be stable that way and bootstrap. As I said, a meditator might say: everything felt bad in my body, but as long as I had a spark of loving-kindness in a tiny part of my body, I could just focus on that. Don't focus on the frustration. Don't focus on the things that are tense. Focus on the parts of your experience that are *relaxed*, and then try to build those. That is apparently really effective. I find it effective — and if you do it a lot on retreat, you can enter these beautiful, hyper-relaxed states of consciousness.

### The Titan realm

What else? Well, we have the **Titans** — also the world of the **asuras**, the jealous gods. They feel everything as an attack. It's unpleasant, but it's *attack-heavy*, very kinetic. The vibes of it are these very sharp attacks. Things actually *hit*: internal representations get blasted, as opposed to being softly, gently interacted with. And they erase information.

Unfortunately, war aesthetics and things like that — the times that we live in — are full of that realm, which is really unfortunate, and it's anti-fragile. We've got to figure out how to disable it. And I think understanding how to disable it with non-linear wave dynamics is probably pretty helpful. I'm very optimistic. That's maybe a way.

Now, the core — the internal representation, who you are when you're in the Titan world — is a kind of weapon depot: all of these non-linear gestalts you've assembled that are strong and powerful and can destroy things. You can point them at things, and they will emit a harsh vibe in that direction.

There's some degree of controllability to it. In the hell realm it's uncontrollable — the gestalts work on their own. In the Titan realm there's some degree of controllability, except that of course the controllability itself is driven by emotions that are hard to control. So how voluntary it is is up for debate.

### The animal realm

Then you have the **animal realm**: ignorance. You just want to veg out and forget about everything, just *be*. A cozy place, simple pleasures, immediate gratifications.

I would describe that realm as very *sticky*. The vibe has a sticky, almost slimy quality. You want to find a cozy part of your experience and just veg out in it. It's not very energized and it's not very intelligent. It's sleepy, it's dumb, it's soporific. It lacks the activation energy to get out of it. And obviously a lot of things can put us in that attractor — attached with a small pressure.

What is inside you — what is the core — when you're in the animal realm? It's a collection of petty likes and dislikes: oh, I like this warmth, I don't like this cold. I like this, I don't like that. And it doesn't go much further; it's not more interesting than that. It's the sort of thing that a fish probably experiences in the core. *Well — depends on the fish and the circumstance. Probably a bad metaphor.* But that would be the animal realm.

### The human realm

The **human realm** is a realm where the internal representations are, generally speaking, fairly *elegant but not very hedonic*. Very complex representations, where the information tracking is very accurate and very detailed, with a lot of capability for complexity. You use gestalts and stack them together in order to form a computing apparatus — these Rube Goldberg machines.

Doing math, for example: mathematicians need to be in the human realm a lot of the time. They might be in the Titan realm some of the time, for sure. But for a lot of the time, they're in the human realm, because they're generating these fairly anhedonic constructions that can stack together and form high-level constructs. And when you have that, the elegant, blissful experiences are going to be when you can reduce a lot of that complexity, or when all of a sudden you have emergent, highly consonant configurations.

Internally, the attitude, the orientation, is towards *calculating* — and it values complexity.

### The god realm

Just to run quickly through the last ones. In the **god realm**, everything is soft and consonant, and synergistically so. That's the thing I was mentioning: if you boost pleasure enough, the pleasures start to interact with one another.

Just as in the hell realm the gestalts interact with each other and form amalgamations that are worse and worse and worse — in the heaven world, in the god realm, the soft, beautiful, pleasant, colorful representations stack together and form these paradises, where there's a spaciousness and freedom and sense of ease that comes with it. And all of that is constructed out of very beautiful vibes at the very beginning, and out of stacking gestalts that are made of very beautiful vibes.

What is inside you when you're in the god realm? There's a cornucopia of sensory delights. You have an attitude that facilitates and enhances pleasure — and that can also be anti-fragile, to an extent. To an extent.

### The last four: voice-hearers, cause-awakened ones, bodhisattvas, Buddhas

The last four are fairly Buddhist in their interpretation.

**Voice-hearers** are people who figure out how to defabricate the world — using Dark Night or equanimity practices in order to defabricate the non-linearities, the gestalts, and ultimately exit the realms. Voice-hearers are those who listen to the teachings of people who know this stuff. So it's a kind of coupling: you have half of yourself in one realm, but you have another foot in hearing somebody who has fully defabricated the world. You're coupled to that person, and that allows you to slowly defabricate your realm.

Then you have the **cause-awakened ones**: people who bootstrapped a defabrication — who figured it out on their own. Arhats and people like that. Here they will have a core defabrication algorithm. It could be vipassanā, it could be certain kinds of samatha breathing exercises, or an attitude, a way of seeing the world — and they bootstrap out of it. If they focus enough on it, all of a sudden they can use that as the self-organizing principle and defabricate the world.

**Bodhisattvas**, I would say, are like soap molecules: one foot in one world, one foot in the other. But they're at a *homeostatic* level, where they've stabilized things — half of them fully defabricated and half fabricated, so they can interface with the other worlds. These are beings, as it were — I mean, people, in a secular interpretation — who live in a homeostatic vibe-control environment. Part of their realm can get out of control for a little bit, but when that happens, they boost the defabrication component, and vice versa. So they can remain engaged, remain empathetic and understanding, and actually engage and interact with the vibes of the world without getting caught up in them.

And finally, the **Buddhas**. Fourth path[^fourth-path] — we've talked about these kinds of individuals. They live in a world that is fully defabricated. They have access to pure consciousness, fully, completely relaxed, completely let go, completely dropping the ball from any tightness, from any of these non-linearities. That would be total vibe control, in a way, and the ability to not be swayed by any gestalt whatsoever.

## Why this matters

All of these realms obviously come from a religious, mystical interpretation. But my claim is that all of it will actually make sense in terms of non-linear wave dynamics, non-linear wave computing. And understanding it that way will allow us to generate technology to facilitate this process, and to not get so caught up in insane realms — which, oftentimes, nobody is making on purpose. They're an emergent effect.

Especially things like the hell realm and the Titan realm are really sticky, because your attempts to get out of them *fortify* them. That's really terrible. And that's one of the reasons why I think, for example, 5-MeO-DMT is so promising. If you're really caught up in one of those realms, 5-MeO-DMT will show you: wow, every move that I have belongs to the matrix of things that reinforces the realm. So actually, I did *not* have an exit. I needed this wave of equanimity to temporarily defabricate it and give me some sense of spaciousness, so I can then bootstrap from there. So I'm very hopeful that that is going to be very, very, very good in the future.

This may also explain the mood effects that different mental or physiological problems have. Heart disease, or stomach upset, or lung cancer, or bone disease, and so on: they will be emitting their particular kinds of harsh vibes, and that can aggregate over time. That's why chronic pain is worse than acute pain — because it's going to be generating these non-linearities. The non-linearities will have a harsh vibe, you will create gestalts, and eventually you will generate a realm, which is your realm of being a sufferer of chronic pain. And that's so bad, so sad and terrible, isn't it?

So I think we can figure all of this out. I'm very optimistic that the nervous system can be fixed, that we can exit this paradigm. I know it's possible, because there are people who don't suffer from all these bugs that our nervous system has. And not only highly advanced meditators — also people who genetically are in a really good mood all the time. I think they probably just don't have harsh vibe generators inside them.

I'm very, very hopeful that we could build a society and move in that direction. It's going to take a lot of work and a lot of sanity. But hopefully we can cultivate it.

And with that — thank you so much for listening and for being here. As I recently wrote in a tweet against social media: live, laugh, love, and maximize valence for all sentient beings.
