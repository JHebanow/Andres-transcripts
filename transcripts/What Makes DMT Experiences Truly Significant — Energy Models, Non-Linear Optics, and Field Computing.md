## A detour through UFOs

But before I go there, I want to talk briefly about UFOs and unidentified aerial phenomena.

This is a topic I used to not take very seriously, for a number of reasons. There are many reasons why you probably shouldn't take it seriously: there's a base rate of people who are histrionic. Some people love to draw attention, to be at the center of the party, to have something interesting happen to them. There are traceable human motivations for claiming to experience very strange things. And there are hallucinations. Self-suggestion is really powerful. Measurements can be wrong. There are a lot of reasons why you might want to simply not trust people's accounts of UFOs.

At the same time, quite a few really smart people are taking a look at this. I recently came across — well, somebody emailed me about it, and I really appreciate that lead — a new organization called the Sol Foundation, which held a launch conference at Stanford last year, organized in part by Garry Nolan, Stanford faculty, who has recently been analyzing "materials from other worlds", from maybe other civilizations.[^sol] I'll explain a bit more in a second. But watching those presentations, and learning more about the history of this, it really does seem that there are very strange things going on in the skies.

I was ticked off by the situation with the New Jersey drones — some of you may know what I'm talking about. Very early on I just thought: somebody must be playing games there, some hobbyist must be putting drones in the sky, and then people are freaking out, and the police send a helicopter to see what's up, and then people report the helicopter, and there are more drone reports, and the hysteria multiplies. For a few days, my priors and my understanding of human nature made me think this was an easily dismissible thing.

However, it really does seem like something very weird is happening, because there are too many witnesses. Too many stories point in the same direction about what type of phenomenon this is. We're talking about something that is probably car-sized, that moves in formations, that appears only at certain hours of the day. But the strangest thing is that they can hover for very, very long times. People in government say they don't know what they are, but that they aren't theirs, and aren't from a foreign adversary.

I scraped a bunch of news about these from verified sources, and I was playing with Claude to try to figure out which conventional explanations might actually account for them. And fortunately — or unfortunately, for my epistemology — none of the conventional explanations seem to make sense, at least not to account for everything, including the incursions into restricted airspace over military installations. But above all there is the very strange phenomenology of it: people report orbs, essentially spheres of light, moving extremely fast.

In the presentations I saw from the Sol Foundation, one researcher in particular, Kevin Knuth, presented interesting analyses of the acceleration of these objects as measured by radar.[^knuth] It all seems to indicate that these things can move at up to 5,000 g, which would completely obliterate any material — no craft that humans would design could endure those accelerations. And what's even stranger is that they don't make a sonic boom, and they can go from air to water without causing a splash. There might be a lot of things going on here.

## The psychology of things we don't understand

But what I want to highlight is that there are things we don't understand. That seems to be the case. And I think it's really interesting to look at the psychology of things we don't understand. Historically, traditionally, human culture takes a while to absorb and make sense of unexplained things — from updating on the shape of the Earth, to accepting that the Sun is at the center of the solar system and that we orbit it rather than the other way around. People are really attached to their priors, to their perceptual filters. So it's not entirely surprising that even when nobody is gating your access to information about very strange things, that information somehow doesn't mobilize a civilization-wide effort to make sense of the strange phenomenon. Look at who ultimately does try to make sense of these things: they tend to be quirky nerds who pay attention to odd things, to what's anomalous in their experience.

Think of optics, for example. When you were a kid and you saw a hologram — if you collected stamps, some of those are holograms, and some Pokémon trading cards have a hologram — it's odd, isn't it? It's almost as though there were an extra axis of space in there. What is going on? My sense is that most kids just make something up and think they understand it, when they actually do not. To understand how a hologram works you need at least some undergraduate courses in physics. The fuller explanation came, at least in my case, from something I highly recommend: reading Feynman's *Lectures on Physics*, or watching his lectures on quantum electrodynamics,[^feynman] which finally articulate all the odd things that light does in terms of a more complete model — one that involves a path integral, the superposition of all possibilities at once, and then taking the square of the summed complex amplitude. If you don't have that sophisticated model, there are a lot of things in optics that just make no sense. And yet people carry on. They go shopping, they go to the movies. For most people it doesn't startle them to the point of really pursuing answers, looking for an explanation, looking for a model that actually fits the whole picture.

## The same blind spot, applied to psychedelics

It turns out I'm quite familiar with this pattern. Why? Because it is absolutely the case with psychedelic states of consciousness. Think about it: such a large percentage of people who take, for example, high-dose DMT report being convinced in the moment that this is not a hallucination, that it is some kind of alien technology, that there is a mind-to-mind transmission with other entities — completely bizarre, very strange content. And yet people's typical reaction is: well, that was just a hallucination. And they laugh it off.

First off: if you take something like DMT experiences seriously, I think it's really important to be able to articulate *why* you take them seriously. And I have a lot of content on this — it's something I've been obsessed with for the last ten years or so, really trying to make sense of what on earth is going on when you take DMT. The answer is not simple, even in the most conventional of explanations. It ends up looking something like — and I'm not saying that it's quantum — something along the lines of quantum electrodynamics, where the model requires quite a bit of fiddling and conceptual openness before you get to something that has explanatory power, rather than just laughing it off because it sounds ridiculous.

## Why the semantic content is the least interesting part

One very big mistake that people make when they talk about their deep psychedelic experiences, unfortunately, is that they focus very much on the semantic content, which is actually the least impressive aspect of it. Why? Because semantic content can be produced in many different ways, and very different processes can arrive at the same description. If I say: I went to a crazy cartoon land where there was a clown that had an ice cream cone, and then I transformed into the ice cream cone, and then I was torn apart in half, and half of me was in some kind of strange fractal bushes and the other half was melting in the sun, and then the experience ended — I haven't really communicated much. Why? Because this could be a dream. It could be a salvia experience, it could be a DMT experience, it could be a ketamine experience, an iboga experience, hypnosis. Nothing that I said would actually narrow down the texture of the experience, or the dynamics, which are what point at useful scientific information about this phenomenon.

So for the most part I don't pay attention to the semantic content. I think that it's fascinating — we do live by narratives and meaning, and that's very significant from a personal perspective. But from a scientific perspective it's the least interesting aspect of these experiences. Instead, I focus on the **phenomenal character**: the what-it-is-like of the experience, in as much detail and precision as possible, ideally grounded in mathematical models. And then, above that, on the dynamics: what kind of thing is the field of consciousness, such that it has these exotic configurations like a DMT trip?

## What is actually strange: hyperbolic geometry

If you want to make progress there, you need to actually characterize what is strange about a DMT experience. One of the first things I would point out is hyperbolic geometry; I have a lot written about this.[^hyperbolic] On DMT you may enter a crazy palace — beautiful, spiritual, sure, and there might be angels or aliens. Again, I would honestly say: disregard the content. That's not what makes it interesting. And maybe the walls are tessellated with heptagons, seven-sided polygons. And yet the walls seem completely flat, without distortions, and they seem to be very large walls.

Hold on a second — this is impossible. It's impossible to tessellate a space with heptagons, at least not in Euclidean space. In Euclidean space you can tessellate with triangles, with squares, and with hexagons, and that's it — at least if you only use one polygon, one shape of polygon. But heptagons you cannot, because at each vertex the angles add up to more than 360 degrees. Meaning that if you actually experienced a crisp, undistorted tessellation of heptagons, you were experiencing a hyperbolic perceptual space. Or at the very least, the fields of your consciousness were shaped in a way that would give rise to implicit hyperbolic geometry in terms of the subjective distances between the elements of the scene.

That already is very peculiar and fascinating. But there are other things as well. There are a lot of optical phenomena. For instance, if one of the things that you hallucinate on DMT is networks of crystals, one of the things that happens is that they can focus light, as it were. It's almost as if you were engaging with a sophisticated physics engine that is usually not activated — or as if you were actually tapping into physics, in a sense. And I tend to suspect it's the latter: that consciousness is the intrinsic side of the fields of physics, which is how I tend to make sense of reality these days. And the type of phenomena that happen on high-dose DMT have to do with the high-energy configurations of these fields of physics, and with the dynamics that that gives rise to.

## Energy models

So let me tell you a little bit more about this. In computer science, in machine learning, and in physics broadly, there are what you call **energy models**. I think that's actually a very promising way of studying any phenomenon at all: just postulate that there are different levels of energy, that different transformations can happen at those levels of energy, and that there are energy sources and energy sinks. And it really does seem that from this perspective you can assign a total level of energy to a moment of experience and reason about its properties from that point of view.

In physics that would be called the **Hamiltonian**: how you add together the features of a physical system in order to come up with the final energy score of that system. The classic example is something like a pendulum. An idealized pendulum, with no air friction, has a constant amount of energy; the energy just goes from potential energy to kinetic energy and back, but the total energy is the same. In other words, the transformations that are happening within that system are taking place in a cross-section of its Hamiltonian, which is all of the possible configurations of the system for a given level of energy.

I think it actually makes a lot of sense to talk about consciousness in terms of levels of energy. Partly because it makes intuitive sense: subjectively, when you're very awake, when things feel very real, you say you're very energized; when you feel very depressed, very sleepy, you say you're low in energy. Okay — let's take that description seriously and try to formalize it. One simple way of quantifying the total amount of energy of your consciousness would be to break your experience into a series of pixels and then add up the intensity of each of those pixels; that sum is the total energy score of your moment of experience.

### The energy source: tracers

From that perspective, DMT dramatically increases the energy of your consciousness. To begin with, it has this **tracer** effect: it takes snapshots of what you just experienced and makes them flicker and linger on top of what you're experiencing now. In a sense this is already happening all the time — we always have a little bit of a tracer on our experience, and I think that's computationally significant. It's perhaps essential to the very notion of conscious memory that we have, and to the sense of the passage of time; you require a little bit of a tracer of what it is that you have been experiencing recently.

On DMT the tracer flickers: it alternates very fast between its color and the opposite color. So if you have a blue ball moving around in your visual field, the tracer will alternate between blue, yellow, blue, yellow, blue, yellow, and it will be doing it very fast, at around 30 hertz or so. This is something that we have measured with QRI's tracer replication tool.[^tracer] And as a function of the dose, the tracer will be longer, and it will also be flickering at a faster frequency. At high doses everything is flickering super fast at lots of different frequencies, all of them pretty high, and they're all trying to interface with each other, to interact with each other.

In that sense, that is a massive energy influx into the system. And then you have **energy sinks**: the ways in which the system is going to try to dissipate that energy. The premise here is that we evolved in such a way that our natural energy-minimization process does computationally useful things — things that natural selection would be recruiting for replication, for causal effects, for information-processing purposes.

### Energy sink one: semantic content

The two main energy sinks to consider, in order to make sense of these experiences, are, first of all, semantic content. Whatever you have experienced before is something that the field can sort of snap into. When something is similar to something you've seen before, you have that trace of a memory of how features are put together, and that crystallizes the field a little bit around it. So if on DMT everything is super wobbly and chaotic, and then you stumble upon — oh, there's a dog — the field may crystallize or wrap around that concept or object, like shrink wrap around something that you recognize. As a consequence, on DMT the field is constantly coalescing and converging on things that you recognize.

And then of course this becomes recursive, because the moment you crystallize into a dog, the dog will add context to the entirety of the scene, which changes the energy landscape for other things that you might recognize. So things that are more related to dogs will be prioritized in your ability to recognize them, and will lower the energy of your field. As a consequence, you may end up in something that has a lot of other dogs — a dog daycare, for example. You may hallucinate a dog daycare, or something along those lines.

I wrote an article called "Aligning DMT Entities" that explores this process a little bit more and compares it to GPT.[^aligning] In the realm of perception, as opposed to in the realm of language and predicting next tokens, you're predicting parts of your field as a function of the information that is embedded in the rest of the field. And you're doing it in a massively parallel, multi-scale way, and it converges very quickly on things that you can recognize or make sense of.

It's actually for that reason that the content of DMT experiences is, to me, not as significant: because that is more related to what you know and what you have experienced — the high-level energy sinks that you use to make sense of the world, so as to not be surprised all the time. And because this is such a strange experience, it doesn't surprise me that people converge on mystical things like angels and aliens, things that are at the edge of our sense of possibility. We're like: well, everything else has failed, so it must be this. I personally interpret that just as a semantic energy sink. And I think it's much more fruitful if we analyze it that way than if we take it at face value right away. What we should take at face value is not that — and I'll explain what it is, and it's much more interesting than that.

### Energy sink two: symmetric configurations

And then the other very important energy sink is symmetric configurations. I've cataloged this very extensively — you can see it in my Harvard talk on the hyperbolic geometry of DMT experiences,[^harvard] and in a bunch of my write-ups. DMT produces different symmetrical patterns as a function of the dose. At smaller doses you get these two-dimensional symmetry groups, which are all the ways you can tessellate a two-dimensional surface with polygons. The patterns on the wall behind me are the 17 wallpaper symmetry groups. We even have t-shirts with them. They're pretty cool.

Essentially, if you look at a wall that has a stochastic texture, your visual field may converge into any one of those 17 symmetry groups. At a slightly higher dose, that gets to those hexagonal or heptagonal tessellations, which are hyperbolic in nature, where the angles add up to more than 360 degrees. If you take higher doses, then you start going into the realm of three-dimensional symmetries. What are called the **space groups** are all of the ways in which you can essentially tessellate space. And there's a whole bunch of them, around 230.

But it doesn't end there. That is still essentially the upper range of the Magic Eye level. The moment you get to the Waiting Room, which is the fourth level, and especially to Breakthrough,[^levels] you actually are able to experience three-dimensional hyperbolic geometry, where, for example, you may experience space itself being tiled with dodecahedra, and things like that. Things that are definitively impossible in Euclidean space.

Now, again, please don't jump to conclusions. I'm not saying that you're entering a literal hyperbolic space outside of your mind, or anything like that. My default assumption is that these are configurations of the field of consciousness within your own topological pocket, generated by your nervous system. But these are the high-energy configurations of that field, which may have very different geometric and topological properties.

So you can catalog that. You can actually use these energy models to analyze, in a sense, what happens in a DMT experience: in terms of how the energy sources interact with the energy sinks, and how the experience ends up being stitched together by combining things that you have recognized before with symmetries, which are physically just natural energy sinks.

## Three things that would actually get my attention

All of that, actually, I figured out on my own, just by thinking very deeply about this and really caring about the phenomenon. But it's in some sense just the basics. DMT turns out to be way more challenging to our conceptions of how consciousness works than just this. And I'll give you a couple of reasons why.

Again, I don't personally focus at all on the semantic content. You could tell me you saw an angel and that it told you Jesus went to Tibet and learned Buddhism — some complex weaving narrative — and I will most likely just make sense of it in terms of your own personal conceptual energy sinks, and how you were stitching together that high-energy configuration of consciousness. So it's not going to surprise me much. But there are things that you can tell me that will absolutely draw my attention. And I'll mention three. These come from very credible sources, and some of them have been reported by more than one person.

They have to do with DMT analyzed from the point of view of **qualia computing**: computation using consciousness, where the computation is really powerful, and doesn't seem to be the sort of thing that us humans are particularly good at. It seems to be tapping into some physical algorithmic process that actually speeds up computation very significantly.

### 1. Coloring surfaces

The first one is coloring, essentially related to the four-color theorem. If you have a map, let's say of a bunch of countries, and you start painting those countries, it's difficult, but it is always possible to paint them in such a way that no two neighbors are painted with the same color. This is called the four-color theorem, which applies to some manifolds — the typical ones that we would encounter. It doesn't apply to, for example, two doughnuts put together, or a pretzel; the numbers are actually different for that type of object. But the point is that for any given surface there's a mathematical guarantee for what is the minimum number of colors that you need in order to paint it in such a way that you don't have neighboring surfaces or regions that are of the same color.[^four-color]

On DMT this can happen in extraordinary ways. You can enter these very complex hyperbolic surfaces, really intricate, maybe in some cases higher-dimensional. And you may just dismiss it when I say higher-dimensional, because — well, what does this guy know about higher dimensions? But no, seriously: we can probably even formalize in what sense this phenomenology is higher-dimensional, including the existence of four-dimensional rotations, for which we actually have a tool that you can play with to get an intuition for what I'm talking about.[^4d] These are very complex shapes, configurations you have never seen before.

And then you may have the phenomenology of "contacting a type of intelligence" that just loves to paint surfaces. And if you "get in touch" with that type of intelligence — or hallucination, or self-organizing principle, whatever it ends up turning out to be — right away it will just paint everything perfectly, and there's going to be no neighboring regions that are of the same color. And this is not done naively; it is not done in a sequential fashion. It seems to be done in a way that is simultaneously aware of the low-level constraints, the local constraints, and the global shape. And it happens extremely fast — within the span of a second or less. And on top of that, it can be done again and again, on top of itself, iterated over. If this is in some sense an intelligence showing you some kind of interesting demonstration, it's able to do it a bunch of times in a row, in a way that is really compelling.

Now, I'm absolutely not jumping in any way to the conclusion that this is an intelligence external to you. But I do think this indicates — to the extent that these reports are credible — that you can actually tap into these exotic self-organizing principles that provide very unusual computational advantages, and that does hint at what kind of substrate consciousness must be running on.

### 2. Projective intelligence and compactifications

The second type of intelligence, or of strange intelligence-like behavior, that happens on high-dose DMT has to do with what we've been calling **projective intelligence** and **compactifications**. On projective intelligence I have a video, one of the most recent ones,[^projective] where I talk about how a lot of what I think our conscious mind is doing is finding mappings between fields of different dimensions in order to compute things with them. This is, by the way, something machine learning is currently studying and in some sense discovering. But I think this is actually essential to how we process information.

To a first approximation, the visual field is two-dimensional and the somatic field is three-dimensional. Every visual sensation in your visual field is implemented with a tiny muscular contraction that keeps track of the embodied sense of distances. I've talked to Roger Thisdell and Wystan Bryant-Scott, people who are very attained meditators, and they confirmed that in very advanced meditation stages the visual field becomes much more harmonized with the somatic field, and the resting state is actually just two-dimensional. And for you to fabricate any sense of depth, you require a little bit of tension. So actually inhabiting a 3D world comes with an inherent sense of a little bit of tension.

In our normal everyday life we're doing this back and forth between three-dimensional representations, two-dimensional representations, and waves that live in either of those two representations. And we're trying to align them in order to minimize out-of-phase interactions.

Here's one of the things that you can do with this type of computation: analyzing an object by spinning it in its space and finding a projection onto your visual field that highlights a particular symmetry. An example of this is looking at a cube. If you have a cube and you can rotate it, you can put one face right in front of you, and then its outline forms a square. The cube is more than a square: the cube is a three-dimensional object, whereas the square is a two-dimensional object that is symmetric in two dimensions — it has rotational symmetry and reflection symmetry within the plane. Meaning that those symmetries are also in the object that you're projecting, but along only one axis. And then you can rotate the cube again and project it differently, and find another square, and then another square. And in that way you can characterize the symmetries of the three-dimensional object by looking at the symmetries of the projection.

Now, the visual field, being a two-dimensional surface, has two-dimensional resonant modes. And so it is very easy for it — again, from an energy-model perspective — to find the inherent symmetries via a process of stochastic resonance, essentially amplifying the resonances that are natural in that space. In other words, it's as if you had this cavity with many possible resonant modes, but all of them two-dimensional in nature. And so if you have a three-dimensional object that you projected into it, then naturally and very easily, cheaply, in a massively parallel way, you can find the two-dimensional symmetries of that three-dimensional object.

I think that we are constantly doing that. Whenever we are mapping out a space, reading a map, deciding how we're going to get to work, or even how we're going to send a basketball to somebody else, we are making projective transformations: mappings between 3D and 2D, keeping track of the symmetries in 2D, remapping them to 3D, modifying them in 3D, reprojecting — constantly. We're constantly doing that. And that is a little bit stressful. How efficiently you do it probably has a lot to do with your kinesthetic intelligence. And even the fluidity of thought might have something to do with the efficiency with which you do this process.

It's already very sophisticated in a normal everyday state of consciousness, but on high-dose DMT it can be so much more sophisticated. I'll just give you one example of what can happen here. There is a type of superintelligence on DMT, recognizable in the sense that if you are, quote-unquote, contacting, merging with, or becoming one with that type of intelligence, it's sort of like you have a profound intuitive understanding of how to do these projective transformations for information-processing purposes. And one of the things that becomes accessible is being able to take entire streams of qualia — a life's story, for example — and somehow, don't ask me how, reproject it in such a way that it becomes a tube, a very thin tube. And then the tube can get painted with color in such a way that the information content inside that tube is completely and fully captured by the paint on the surface.

It's holographically painted, in such a way that by peering into that tube you can see the entire stream of sensations, but it can also be abstracted away by looking at it from a distance. And then what you can actually do is compare a bunch of these tubes together and do very quick operations by shifting the colors around and making comparisons. And in a matter of seconds, maybe a minute, arrive at very sophisticated judgments of similarities and differences and isomorphisms and commonalities between, let's say, the life stories of two completely different characters, in a way that is self-evident. And once you find those resonances, you can recover them by refolding or unfolding this tube back into its life stream.

So what is going on there? Sure, you can say it's a hallucination, no problem. But is it trivial from an information-processing perspective? I don't think so. I think this probably contains, at the very least, powerful hints for how to advance machine learning in completely novel directions. But I think it's more profound than that. I think it's actually hinting at how to generalize this projective intelligence that is already naturally available to us to higher dimensions and alternative geometries. I don't know what we can do with this yet. But to me it's obvious that if you're smart and paying attention, this should be a highlight. It should be: okay, oh gosh — as a humanity, as a civilization, we do not understand this. This is a little bit of a paradigm-shifting or framework-challenging feature of reality that ought to be studied if we're curious about consciousness and reality.

### 3. Finding topological extrema

The third one that I will mention is the finding of **topological extrema**. This also strongly suggests energy models. The metaphor here is with a pear, effectively. If you have a pear — one of those pears that have a kind of neck — and you take a string, and you make a loop with that string around it, and then you slowly try to shrink that string, it's going to find a bottleneck: the place where that distance, the amount of string needed to go around the pear, is minimized. That is a topological extremum. It's a kind of shortest path embedded in that shape. Finding this is computationally challenging. If all I give you is a mesh of that pear — there are algorithms to do it, but many of them, for example, use spectral graph theory and a lot of things that are massively parallel in nature, and they can actually find this extremum, but it's not obvious right away. It's something for which you need to do some significant work.

DMT states of consciousness seem to be able to do this automatically, as just a feature of that state, and for very complex higher-dimensional shapes. There might be a very crazy surface, or a field of awareness that is twisted in a certain way, and right away you find the shortest paths within it. And there might be multiple ones. They might be orthogonal to each other, moving in different directions, and they weave together — it could be a very exotic topology. That's actually one of the highlights of a breakthrough DMT experience: it tends to involve changes to the topology of your field of experience, where it stops feeling like you're just looking at a volume from a certain perspective, and it's more like — no, you are a kind of Möbius strip, or a Klein bottle, but a higher-dimensional version of it. And it may manifest in ways such as, for example, connecting your embodied sense of your toe to your forehead. And then you're embodying a cartwheel. It's not necessarily pleasant, it's very strange, but it is very exotic. It's a very exotic configuration of the field of consciousness.

And what's fascinating — and this is from sources that I trust, who I think have the technical expertise to be able to evaluate this phenomenon — is that indeed, right away, the state highlights all of those minima, all of those geodesics. Again, to me this suggests an energy model. It suggests something that uses path integrals, that is exploring all possibilities at once and putting them in superposition. It suggests a certain type of computational medium, which to me puts in relief **non-linear optics** as a strong broad candidate for the possible physical substrate of how conscious experience is implemented.

## Hive minds, Indra's net, and the temple

And there's so much more. There are also more "superintelligences" that exist in DMT states of consciousness. There seem to be things that are these geometry beings, higher-dimensional hyperbolic geometry beings. To give you a flavor of what we're talking about: imagine a Christmas tree where there are a lot of ornaments, and all of those ornaments are reflecting all of the other ones, so all of them have a tiny model of the rest. And usually they're organized in a very crisp hierarchical way, with a star at the top. Maybe that is like our normal mind, where we have a model at the top — which is our sense of self — that is keeping a snapshot of the state of the entire system, while every part of the system also has a model of at the very least the other parts it's interacting with, in order to minimize prediction errors, to cut down on the entropy cost and the heat that this computational system might be generating. And it's finely tuned for normal everyday tasks.

In the DMT state of consciousness, imagine now that there are 20 of these stars, and they're all competing for being the center, in a sense. 5-MeO-DMT is different, in that there is no central star. There is no center, and there's no competition for being the center — or everywhere is the center. DMT, not really. DMT has a lot of egos, as it were. It has a lot of centers. But then collectively they can coalesce into something that seems capable of computations of a higher order than what we are normally capable of.

And when you have something like this — I've been calling them not only **Indra's net**, which is this concept of everything reflecting everything else, but more like Indra's hypercomputer neural net,[^indra] because it is also a supercomputer. And it is able to understand itself and model itself as a whole. So there's a model of how everything is put together, but also every part of it is keeping track of specific information.

That whole system may process something like a trauma. That sounds completely ridiculous — using this massive supercomputer to process something like the fact that you felt left out in elementary school because they bullied you, or something like that. Well, sometimes that happens. Sometimes one of these things is what helps you process that sort of information. But it could be something entirely different. It could be trying to make sense of an equation, for example, or trying to make sense of the concept of time, using these sorts of computational architectures.

But what's really stunning is the agentivity — how agentic and coordinated these kinds of hive minds can be. They can, for example, turn into a temple. They can see each other and coordinate. You know when you're in a circle of people and everybody's holding hands, but you're in a strange contorted position, and you say: okay, at the count of three we will all expand our hands, our arms — and everybody does it at the same time, in a coordinated fashion, and if you see it from above, you see how this weird mesh of people organizes into just a perfect circle. They can do things like that, where they are measuring each other's position and configuration, how they are put together. And then they realize an opportunity: wow, we could actually turn ourselves into this beautiful temple full of rainbows, and it looks like classic Jain architecture, or something like that. ## Energy as invariance: jhānas and conserved quantities

Anyway — just some thoughts on the matter. Energy models.

Oh, there's one last thing I wanted to communicate about energy models, and that is — I mean, this is a deep rabbit hole — but energy also has the sense of identifying invariants. Take meditative energy, jhāna energy, in the high-concentration states of consciousness that you can access via meditation. Why call that *energy* as opposed to something like *activation*? Well, because if you're actually experiencing these very beautiful, harmonious traveling waves, you will notice that one of their features is that they don't change in their shape as they move around. And that actually connects with physical energy: the thing that makes physical energy what it is is the symmetries of space and time, the symmetries of physics. The fact that there is symmetry over space, or over time, is the reason why there is preservation of momentum, preservation of energy.[^noether]

So, in the same way, this connects to the **symmetry theory of valence**,[^stv] and it connects to **neural annealing**.[^annealing] If you can set up your system in such a way that the cardiovascular system, the peripheral nerves, maybe the lymphatic system — all of these other auxiliary components of your being — are resonating in phase in the right way, and they're instantiating a field-like behavior, then it actually makes quite a lot of sense to talk about this in terms of energy, because you're talking about in what way you have invariant shapes: shapes that don't distort as they travel. And that does seem to be a general pattern: that as you advance further in the jhānas, in concentration, you're tapping into new invariants, you're tapping into new ways of homogenizing your field of experience in such a way that patterns can multiply and survive across space and time within your pocket of experience.

I'll just give you one more interesting pointer, which is angular momentum. When you have a planet orbiting a star, the reason angular momentum is preserved is the rotational symmetry of that system. This actually applies to the jhānas, applies to meditation states as well. Because you can either homogenize your field in such a way that you have, let's say, Euclidean symmetries, where the patterns repeat in a way that is translationally symmetrical; but you can also enter into these more circular jhānas, where there's a point at the center, and everything is the same as you go away from that point. And if you can actually make it perfectly rotationally symmetric, that also activates this invariant, and the "free" — with no dissipation — movement of waves in a rotational way.

And the more complex things — which I think definitely happen at higher doses of DMT — are that there might be patches where you have Euclidean symmetries, patches where you have hyperbolic symmetries, spherical geometries. And in what way the waves can travel without dissipating and without changing shape depends on the symmetries of the patch of the field of consciousness that you're examining. So to a large extent, a lot of the things that I think happen on DMT have a lot to do with what the local geometric invariants of the different regions of your consciousness are — in such a way that different notions of energy become possible, and you can have a kind of algebra of interactions between those different patches of your field.
