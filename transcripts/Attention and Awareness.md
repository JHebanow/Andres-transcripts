## What are attention and awareness?

There's also this bubbling up of awareness becoming something that is structured into actual perceptual objects, through some kind of process of quasi-attention. So what's going on there?

In this video, we're going to generalize the concept of attention from just a flashlight into something far more general and trippy — but also, I think, more explanatory.

Now, in cognitive science and neuroscience, attention is somewhat fairly well defined.[^scholarpedia] You can, for example, train a monkey to pay attention to something that they're not looking at, by rewarding them: you put a hint over here, and if they don't move their eyes but can still notice the information in the periphery and relate that information, they get a banana or something like that. So you can train them to pay attention to things that they're not looking at — to not fixate on them with their fovea, with the center of their visual field. That's one of the things that neuroscience is actually really good at: taking one sample of people doing one task and another sample of people doing a different task, doing neuroimaging on them, then using a black-box machine learning algorithm to tell you which one is which. And then they look at the features, at which ones are most predictive, and conclude: okay, probably the insula is involved, or the temporal lobe is involved, or something like that. And that's your paper. That's a very typical, formulaic way of producing research in academia. Of course, there's a lot more to it, and this is a cartoon picture — but to a first approximation.

And that kind of study doesn't really tell you what attention *is*, does it? All it is telling you is that the information about where you're paying attention is readable with neuroimaging. It's not actually telling you what attention is at a more fundamental level.

And when you do that and you amp up the concentration, that's going to anneal and override the imperfections. And you're going to get a smooth geometrical space that contains the vibe of the thing you're paying attention to — again, because they co-arise. If you're paying attention to love, it's not only love that is going to be in your attention: love is going to be propagated in your awareness field, or at least to the extent that you're concentrated, to the extent that you're focused on it.

But there are also these very exotic things, where you can actually turn awareness into attention and attention into awareness. Let's say you're focusing on this point very heavily: everything is oscillating with it, and it is the oscillatory complement to the rest of your experience. And then you start to expand and expand it: the region that is the oscillatory complement of your experience becomes larger and larger, until you get to this critical threshold where half of your experiential field is the complement of the other half of your experiential field. And then you can start to narrow down the size of the complementary field — the oscillatory complement on the other side — until you get a fixed point *there*.

So, isn't that bizarre? That actually means that the awareness became the attention, and the attention became the awareness. And you can do that in meditation, and it's really trippy. I think that non-dual states like the ones you experience on LSD, or on higher doses of concentration in meditation, are when you actually find this perfect balance, where neither of the two sides is something you can say is more narrow than the other.

All of a sudden, that breaks the paradigm of the flesh — sorry, the *flashlight* of attention (*though of course, we are made of flesh, so, okay, it's fine*). It breaks the paradigm because you can actually expand it out. And all of a sudden, you have something where neither of them is privileged.

So, my understanding is that when people fall in love with this idea of the balance between good and evil, it's just that they have this internal representation where good and evil are balanced. That just feels good, because it's a high-valence configuration — but it's an illusion. It's not actually representing something that is coherent at a more semantic level. And once you understand that that's the trick, you stop. It's like: okay, fine, I was kidding myself. Suffering is bad. I'm just coping — just coping with the existing suffering. The struggle is real. The struggle is real.

## Attention management as a pro-social skill

So, I guess to conclude: I think attention management is an important pro-social skill, in a way. And unfortunately, you have these arms races in communities. There's this fun presentation — I think I did a transcript of it on Qualia Computing — about virtue signaling, runaway virtue signaling in EA, and things like that.[^signaling] Essentially, when something has the power to catch attention, people start to compete in creating more attention-grabbing versions of it.

That's actually one of the reasons why I don't think it's very wise to engage with something like the existential risk community. If you want to actually help with the existential risk effort, you probably need to find super smart people, make a circle of trust, and just talk about those ideas inside it. Because when it's distributed, decentralized — and there are a lot of things for which decentralization is great, actually, but not everything — I think decentralized x-risk brainstorming is a terrible idea. Because all of a sudden, you have this community where everybody's trying to find the most attention-grabbing way of destroying the world in order to prove their own worth. It's like: hey, I came up with this creative way of destroying the world, and I'm going to write a post on the EA Forum, and hopefully I will be able to be hired into one of these x-risk organizations. And sure, you demonstrated your value and your ability and your skill set — but you also added information on how to destroy the world. I'm actually really worried about that dynamic. There's a bunch of things that I don't talk about precisely for that reason.

So this is very important. I think attention management as a pro-social skill is important to be aware of at a metacognitive level: okay, is this attention dynamic healthy for me, for others? And I would love to develop that sensibility — I'm not saying that I have it — to develop it within me, and within a community where we're aware of how we are playing with each other's attention, and where we're really respectful, so that we can actually create a collective oscillatory complement of each other that generates value, as opposed to zero-sum competitions or whatever it may be.

So they could be made of loving-kindness — this balanced energy of attention and awareness blending into each other. It's kind of like they're loving each other.

And I would be very interested in figuring out how to do that reliably. I've definitely experienced it. It's really cool. Very beautiful. So hopefully we can make more of that.
